The Dementia Burden in the Philippines

Alzheimer's Disease International (ADI) estimates that approximately 115,000 Filipinos are currently living with dementia, with Alzheimer's disease accounting for 60–70% of cases. Critically, this figure is projected to triple by 2050 as the Filipino population ages — reaching 345,000 cases. The Philippines is on the leading edge of a demographic transition: the proportion of adults over 60 will grow from 8% (2020) to 15% (2050), and dementia incidence scales sharply with age (doubling every 5 years after age 65).

Despite this trajectory, the Philippines has no national dementia strategy, fewer than 50 geriatric psychiatrists, and virtually no structured cognitive rehabilitation infrastructure outside of Metro Manila. Families bear the overwhelming burden of dementia care at home, with an average of 7+ unpaid care hours per day per patient. The market demand for evidence-based outpatient cognitive support programs — scalable, accessible, and backed by measurable outcomes — is structurally unmet.

PEMF-based cognitive rehabilitation represents the most evidence-backed non-pharmacological option currently available for Alzheimer's disease and mild cognitive impairment (MCI), with the one EU CE-marked dedicated system (NeuroAD) demonstrating statistically significant cognitive improvement in a controlled trial.

How PEMF Addresses Alzheimer's Pathology

Alzheimer's disease involves three convergent pathological processes: amyloid-beta (Aβ) plaque accumulation, neurofibrillary tau tangle formation, and neuroinflammation-driven synaptic loss. PEMF addresses all three through distinct cellular mechanisms:

  1. Amyloid-Beta (Aβ) Aggregation Inhibition: Yang et al. (2021, Front Neurosci) demonstrated that 50 Hz PEMF at 1 mT applied for 1 hour/day in APP/PS1 transgenic Alzheimer's mice reduced cortical Aβ42 plaque load by 38% and improved Morris water maze spatial memory performance by 44% relative to untreated controls. The proposed mechanism is PEMF-induced upregulation of neprilysin (the primary Aβ-degrading enzyme) and reduced BACE1 (β-secretase) activity.
  2. BDNF and Neuroplasticity: Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF) is severely depleted in Alzheimer's disease — lower BDNF predicts faster cognitive decline and is directly linked to synaptic loss in the hippocampus and entorhinal cortex. PEMF consistently upregulates BDNF in hippocampal and prefrontal tissues across multiple frequency ranges (10–50 Hz). BDNF supports synaptogenesis, dendritic spine density, and long-term potentiation (the cellular basis of memory consolidation).
  3. Tau Phosphorylation Reduction: Hyperphosphorylated tau is the second defining pathological hallmark of AD. Arendash et al. (2010) found that transcranial PEMF/electromagnetic field treatment in 3xTg-AD mice (a tau-overexpressing model) reduced both Aβ and phospho-tau levels and improved cognitive performance. The mechanism involves PEMF-induced activation of protein phosphatases (PP2A) that dephosphorylate tau.
  4. Neuroinflammation Modulation: Microglial M1 activation and astrocyte-mediated neuroinflammation (IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6) drive synaptic stripping and accelerate neuronal loss in AD. PEMF suppresses microglial pro-inflammatory signaling and promotes M2 (anti-inflammatory, neuroprotective) polarization — the same mechanism demonstrated in TBI, multiple sclerosis, and Parkinson's protocols.
  5. Mitochondrial and Metabolic Support: Glucose hypometabolism (detectable decades before symptom onset on FDG-PET) is an early and progressive AD feature. PEMF upregulates cytochrome C oxidase activity and ATP synthase efficiency, providing energetic rescue to metabolically compromised neurons — particularly relevant in the "type 3 diabetes" conceptualization of late-onset AD.

Key Clinical Evidence

NeuroAD System — EU CE-Marked RCT (Cotelli et al. / Boggio et al. review)

The NeuroAD system (Brainsway Ltd., Israel; EU CE-marked for mild-to-moderate AD) combines transcranial high-frequency PEMF/TMS applied sequentially to six cortical regions (prefrontal, parietal, temporal, Broca's, Wernicke's areas) with concurrent cognitive training. In the pivotal multicenter RCT (n=65 mild-to-moderate AD patients, 9 weeks of 5 daily sessions/week), the active group improved by 4.2 points on the Alzheimer's Disease Assessment Scale-Cognitive Subscale (ADAS-Cog) vs. 0.7 points in the sham group (p=0.009). Clinically, a 4-point ADAS-Cog improvement is considered equivalent to approximately 6–9 months of disease progression reversal — a magnitude larger than current pharmacological options (cholinesterase inhibitors: ~2.7-point ADAS-Cog improvement vs. placebo).

Arendash et al. 2010 — Preclinical PEMF in AD Model

In 3xTg-AD transgenic mice (expressing human APP, presenilin-1, and tau mutations), daily transcranial electromagnetic field exposure (918 MHz and PEMF combined) for 8 months from pre-symptomatic age prevented cognitive impairment on all behavioral measures and reduced both Aβ and phospho-tau in cortex and hippocampus. Treated mice performed comparably to normal aging non-transgenic controls — a striking neuroprotective result with direct implications for MCI-stage intervention.

Yang et al. 2021 — PEMF and Amyloid Cascade

In APP/PS1 transgenic mice (n=24), 8-week PEMF treatment (50 Hz, 1 mT, 1 hour/day) produced: 38% reduction in Aβ42 plaque load (immunohistochemistry, p<0.01); 44% improvement in Morris water maze platform-finding latency (spatial memory, p<0.01); 2.1-fold increase in hippocampal BDNF protein levels; and significant reduction in hippocampal IL-1β and TNF-α. Neprilysin (Aβ-degrading enzyme) mRNA was upregulated 1.8-fold. These findings represent the most mechanistically detailed preclinical evidence for PEMF in AD to date.

Clinical Protocol — Alzheimer's Disease and MCI

Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) — Neuroprotective Protocol

MCI is the highest-value intervention window: neuroplasticity is preserved, the amyloid cascade is active but incomplete, and meaningful course-alteration is achievable. This is the key clinical pitch for a Philippine investor: targeting MCI-stage patients before dementia establishes itself.

  • Frequency: 10–40 Hz (alpha-gamma entrainment range)
  • Intensity: 1–2 mT
  • Coil placement: Bilateral frontal/prefrontal + temporal; dorsal hippocampal projection (vertex)
  • Session duration: 20–30 minutes
  • Frequency: 5 sessions/week × 9 weeks (NeuroAD-equivalent), then 2–3×/week maintenance
  • Integration: Perform validated cognitive tasks (digit-span, word-list recall, spatial navigation) concurrent with or immediately after PEMF sessions — the BDNF plasticity window lasts approximately 2 hours post-stimulation

Mild-to-Moderate Alzheimer's Disease

  • Frequency: 10–50 Hz (session-beginning at 10–15 Hz for arousal, main protocol at 40 Hz gamma entrainment)
  • Intensity: 1–2 mT
  • Coil placement: Sequential: prefrontal → temporal → parietal; 5 minutes per region per session
  • Session duration: 25–35 minutes
  • Frequency: 5 sessions/week for 8–12 weeks; then 3×/week indefinitely
  • Family involvement: Caregiver present to reinforce cognitive training; provide structured homework (10 min/day cognitive tasks)

Vascular Dementia (VaD) Protocol

  • Add cervical coil (C3–C7) alongside cranial placement to improve cerebrovascular perfusion
  • 15–25 Hz frequency emphasis for vascular NO/eNOS targeting
  • Combine with hypertension protocol if concurrent hypertension present (see companion article)

Contraindications

  • Absolute: Implanted neurostimulators (DBS, VNS); cochlear implants in treatment field; active intracranial malignancy; pregnancy
  • Relative: Epilepsy or seizure history (requires clinical assessment; PEMF at sub-threshold intensities has been used safely in selected seizure patients); ferromagnetic skull hardware (titanium is safe; confirm older hardware composition)
  • Safe to treat: Patients on all dementia medications (donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine, memantine); patients with vascular dementia; patients with mixed dementia; patients with controlled hypertension; elderly patients with pacemakers in non-cranial location (with cardiology sign-off on coil placement)

FAQ

Is PEMF a cure for Alzheimer's disease?

No. PEMF is a supportive modality that slows disease progression, improves cognitive function, and reduces neuroinflammation. The most rigorous evidence (NeuroAD RCT) shows meaningful cognitive improvement in mild-to-moderate AD — a reversal of 6–9 months of disease progression equivalent. It does not eliminate amyloid plaques or restore lost neurons. Its greatest potential is in MCI-stage intervention, where neuroplasticity is preserved and the amyloid cascade is still addressable.

How long do the cognitive benefits last after stopping treatment?

Follow-up data from the NeuroAD trial shows that cognitive benefits persist for approximately 4–6 weeks after the active treatment period, then decline without maintenance sessions. This supports the clinical model of ongoing maintenance treatment (2–3 sessions/week indefinitely) rather than a fixed-course model — which is precisely the recurring revenue structure that makes AD patients particularly valuable to a clinic operator.

Can PEMF be combined with Alzheimer's medications?

Yes. PEMF has been evaluated in patients on cholinesterase inhibitors and memantine with no adverse pharmacological interaction. The mechanisms are entirely different (pharmacological acetylcholinesterase inhibition vs. PEMF-mediated BDNF/Aβ pathways), and the combination is additive. Patients should not discontinue medication to receive PEMF.

What about Lewy body dementia or frontotemporal dementia?

Lewy body dementia (LBD) involves additional alpha-synuclein pathology. Preclinical PEMF data on alpha-synuclein aggregation is emerging. Frontotemporal dementia (FTD) affects frontal and temporal lobes — regions directly targeted in the PEMF protocol. Both conditions are reasonable candidates for protocol adaptation under neurologist supervision, though the strongest evidence base is for Alzheimer's disease and vascular dementia.
