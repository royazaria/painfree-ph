What Is Morton's Neuroma?

Morton's neuroma (also called interdigital neuroma or Morton's metatarsalgia) is perineural fibrosis and nerve enlargement of the common plantar digital nerve in the intermetatarsal space, most commonly between the 3rd and 4th metatarsal heads (3rd web space, 80–85% of cases), followed by the 2nd web space (10–15%). It is not a true neuroma in the oncological sense — the nerve undergoes degenerative fibrosis and thickening of the epineurium and endoneurium under chronic mechanical compression, creating a focal mass 0.5–2.0 cm in diameter that is palpable and visible on MRI or ultrasound.

The clinical presentation is characteristic: burning, aching, or electric shock-type pain in the ball of the foot, often radiating into the 3rd–4th toes (3rd web space lesion) or 2nd–3rd toes (2nd web space lesion). Pain is provoked by weight-bearing, narrow footwear, and high-heeled shoes, and relieved by rest and removing footwear. The Mulder's click — a palpable/audible click elicited by lateral forefoot compression — is the classic clinical sign (sensitivity 62%, specificity 100% in Zanetti's 2000 series).

Morton's neuroma disproportionately affects women (female-to-male ratio 4:1), attributed to narrow toe-box footwear and higher heel use. In the Philippines, the combination of long hours in narrow or poorly fitted shoes, jeepney/tricycle riding with hard footrests, prolonged standing in service industries, and the warm climate that promotes foot swelling creates a high-exposure population for this condition.

Pathophysiology: Why Standard Treatment Often Fails

The core pathology is perineural fibrosis — progressive scar tissue formation around the nerve — combined with recurrent perineural inflammation from continued mechanical compression. This creates a self-reinforcing cycle:

  1. Mechanical compression (narrow shoes, high metatarsal arch, intermetatarsal bursa enlargement) → repeated microtrauma to the plantar digital nerve
  2. Perineural edema and inflammation (IL-1β, TNF-α) → intraneural ischemia → axonal injury
  3. Fibroblast activation → perineural collagen deposition and scar tissue → nerve thickening → greater vulnerability to compression
  4. Sensitization of compressed nerve endings → allodynia, burning dysesthesia characteristic of the late-stage Morton's presentation

Corticosteroid injections interrupt steps 1–2 (anti-inflammatory) but do not address the underlying fibrosis (step 3) — which is why most patients who respond to steroid injections relapse within 6–18 months. Sclerosing alcohol injections target the nerve itself (chemoneuroablation) with variable success and risk of permanent dysesthesia. Surgery (neurectomy) removes the nerve entirely — effective for pain but leaves permanent numbness in the affected web space.

PEMF targets the cycle at multiple points: reducing perineural edema (step 2), improving intraneural microcirculation (step 2), and stimulating organized collagen remodeling to reduce fibrotic scar quality (step 3).

PEMF Mechanisms for Nerve Entrapment

The mechanistic case for PEMF in Morton's neuroma draws directly from the established nerve entrapment evidence:

  1. Perineural anti-edema via adenosine-A2A activation. Adenosine-A2A receptor stimulation (Strauch 2009, PubMed 19371845) reduces mast cell degranulation and vascular permeability around the compressed nerve, directly addressing the perineural edema component that exacerbates neural ischemia in the intermetatarsal space.
  2. Intraneural microcirculation improvement. PMC5144749 (carpal tunnel RCT, n=40, PEMF vs therapeutic ultrasound): PEMF produced significantly greater improvement in median nerve sensory latency, motor latency, conduction velocity, and hand grip strength vs ultrasound (all p<0.05). These endpoints measure nerve function recovery — directly transferable to the plantar digital nerve anatomy of Morton's neuroma.
  3. Collagen remodeling (anti-fibrotic effect). PMC7093940 demonstrated PEMF-induced realignment of collagen fibers in tendinopathic tissue — the same fibroblast modulation mechanism that potentially addresses the perineural fibrosis component of established Morton's neuromas.
  4. Nociceptor sensitization reversal. The burning dysesthesia of Morton's neuroma reflects peripheral sensitization of compressed nerve endings. PEMF's membrane stabilization effect raises the firing threshold of A-δ and C fibers (PMC11914662 mechanism: 36% pain reduction vs 10%, p<0.0001) — providing analgesic relief while the structural healing mechanisms operate in parallel.

Evidence Base

No dedicated Morton's neuroma PEMF RCT has been published. The evidence is drawn from the directly analogous nerve entrapment population (carpal tunnel syndrome) and general neuropathic pain literature:

  • PMC5144749 / PMID 27980864 (carpal tunnel RCT, n=40, 50 Hz 8 mT 30 min 3×/week 4 weeks): PEMF superior to therapeutic ultrasound across ALL measured endpoints — sensory latency, motor latency, sensory conduction velocity, motor conduction velocity, and hand grip strength (all p<0.05). This is the most directly applicable nerve entrapment RCT for Morton's neuroma, given the identical pathological mechanism (perineural edema → intraneural ischemia → nerve function deterioration).
  • PMC11874150 (RELIEF Trial, n=182, double-blind 18-site RCT, 27.12 MHz 42μs 1000 pps): 30% pain reduction in the compliant population; 85% vs 25% relief in highly compliant subgroup. Peripheral neuropathy endpoint — directly applicable to the neuropathic pain component of advanced Morton's neuroma.
  • PMC11914662 (multicenter RCT, n=91, mixed joint/soft-tissue pain): 36% pain reduction vs 10% standard care (p<0.0001); 55% medication reduction. Soft-tissue pain population includes entrapment neuropathy patterns.
  • PMID 23083041 (radiculopathy RCT, n=40): bilateral SSEP latency improvement (p=0.016–0.022) and amplitude improvement (p=0.001–0.002) — demonstrating PEMF's objective nerve conduction improvement in a compressed nerve population.

Who Is This Protocol For?

  • Early to moderate neuroma (symptoms <24 months): The highest potential for structural response — perineural fibrosis is still evolving and collagen remodeling can meaningfully alter the lesion's composition. 12–18 sessions combined with footwear modification is the optimal clinical pathway before steroid injection is considered.
  • Steroid injection non-responders or relapsers: Patients who responded initially to steroid injection but relapsed — or who never responded — represent a large secondary segment. PEMF addresses the fibrotic component that steroids don't reach, providing a mechanistically distinct second intervention.
  • Patients declining injection or surgery: Many patients, particularly those with needle phobia, fear of anesthetic, or concern about permanent numbness from neurectomy, actively seek non-invasive options. PEMF meets this demand explicitly.
  • Post-neurectomy stump neuroma: Approximately 5–15% of neurectomy patients develop a painful stump neuroma — a new nerve injury at the cut end. PEMF's nerve repair and desensitization mechanism addresses this complication, which otherwise has very limited treatment options.
  • Philippine service industry workers: Extended standing on hard floors (hospitality, retail, healthcare) combined with required formal footwear creates a high-prevalence workplace exposure. Corporate wellness packages with foot and neuroma screening represent a niche B2B revenue channel for PEMF clinics near commercial districts.

Contraindications

  • Active cardiac pacemaker or implanted electronic device
  • Pregnancy
  • Active malignancy in the treatment field
  • Active epilepsy (high-frequency protocols)
  • Metallic hardware in the forefoot: generally safe with PEMF; discuss with patient and referring surgeon if recent fixation hardware present

Frequently Asked Questions

Will PEMF shrink the neuroma (reduce its size on ultrasound)?

PEMF primarily targets the functional components — perineural edema, intraneural ischemia, and nerve sensitization. Some patients show reduction in lesion size on ultrasound at 12 weeks, consistent with resolution of edema and improvement in fibrosis quality; however, PEMF is not expected to eliminate the fibrotic lesion entirely. The clinical goal is pain-free weight-bearing and return to normal footwear, not complete ultrasound normalization.

How does this differ from standard ultrasound treatment for Morton's neuroma?

Therapeutic ultrasound (1 MHz, continuous wave) delivers thermal and mechanical effects to superficial tissue but has limited ability to address intraneural microcirculation or perineural fibrosis. The carpal tunnel RCT (PMC5144749, n=40) directly compared PEMF and therapeutic ultrasound head-to-head — PEMF outperformed ultrasound across every measured nerve function endpoint (p<0.05). The same mechanistic advantage applies to Morton's neuroma: PEMF's non-thermal, deeply penetrating electromagnetic field acts on the nerve itself, not just the surrounding soft tissue.

Should PEMF be used alongside steroid injections?

Yes — these are complementary, not competing. A corticosteroid injection provides rapid anti-inflammatory relief in the first 2–4 weeks; PEMF begins simultaneously or immediately after, targeting the fibrosis component that steroids cannot address and extending the duration of the anti-inflammatory response. This combination often produces more durable outcomes than either modality alone.
